WCAG AA web accessibility in Mexico: guide for organizations

An accessible page is not only a good intention. It is a digital experience that allows more people to understand, navigate, compare, buy, learn or complete procedures without unnecessary barriers.

9 min readWCAG AADigital inclusion

In Mexico, web accessibility is still often treated as a technical pending item: something reviewed when the website is already designed, developed and published. That approach is expensive because teams end up rebuilding components, rewriting content and redesigning flows.

The better approach is to turn accessibility into a continuous process of diagnosis, prioritization, remediation and evidence. Accessibility then becomes an operating capability, not a last-minute correction.

The question is not whether the site looks good. The question is whether a person with a visual, auditory, motor or cognitive disability — or an older adult — can use it independently.

What WCAG AA means

WCAG, the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, is the most widely used international standard for digital accessibility. Level AA is the common reference point for organizations that need a serious balance between rigor, feasibility and impact.

Common issues

  • Buttons or links identified only by color.
  • Low text contrast on pale backgrounds or images.
  • Menus and forms that do not work well with a keyboard.
  • Important images without useful alternative text.
  • Forms that do not explain errors clearly.
  • Disordered heading hierarchies that make screen-reader navigation difficult.

A serious diagnosis

A strong accessibility review combines automation, manual inspection and assistive-technology testing. Automated tools are useful, but they cannot fully evaluate keyboard flows, alternative-text quality, user understanding or real task completion.

What to fix first

Prioritize barriers that block critical tasks: contacting, buying, registering, searching information, downloading documents, completing forms or understanding requirements.

Why it is also a business decision

Accessibility expands your potential audience, improves user experience and can strengthen technical SEO. A semantic, well-structured and clear website is easier for people, search engines and assistive technologies to understand.

Continuous improvement

Accessibility should not end with a report. Audit templates and components, correct severe blockers, document accessible patterns, train content and technology teams, and reassess whenever new campaigns or pages are launched.
